Auctions with Risk Reserves for Scarce AI Research

When portfolio managers compete for four premium AI research slots, when does one verified-risk reserve justify the private value it displaces?

The asset-manager decision

During earnings season, 20 risk-neutral portfolio managers compete for four identical premium AI research slots. Each PM privately knows the value of access. The risk team freezes a public risk score before bidding.

1

FCFS

The first four requests in an exogenous random arrival order receive slots. Payment is zero.

Non-price baseline
2

Pure first-price auction

All four slots go to the highest sealed bids. Each winner pays their own bid.

Private-value benchmark
3

Hybrid

The highest-risk request with r ≥ 60 receives one free slot and exits. Three remaining slots are auctioned. If no request qualifies, all four return to auction. Ties in risk scores or bids are broken by fixed PM ID.

Shared research question

Can a hybrid mechanism that combines auction-based allocation with reserved capacity for objectively high-risk research requests improve the allocation of scarce AI research capacity relative to a pure first-price auction?

Two different bid rules: b=0.8v is an assumed multi-slot simulation rule, not an equilibrium result. The exact b(v)=0.75v BNE applies only to the separate four-bidder, single-slot behavioral benchmark.
Decision rule: For hybrid H and pure first-price auction P, let ΔC = C(H) − C(P) and L = V(P) − V(H). The hybrid improves the stated firm index exactly when λΔC > L.

Fixed-payoff framing round

This is a separate one-slot first-price auction with four symmetric bidders. It tests whether people bid like the rational first-price benchmark, especially whether loss framing moves bids away from it. It does not directly test Hybrid versus Pure. Under risk neutrality and independent uniform private values, the benchmark is b(v)=3v/4.

Experimental validity: Gain and Loss treatments hold private value, opponent-value distribution, winning rule, payment rule, and numerical payoff constant. Only the wording changes: same economic payoff + different framing.

Methods and sources

Three connected lenses

  • Economics defines incentives and the value–risk trade-off.
  • Computation compares matched allocations and sensitivity conditions.
  • Behavioral science probes whether fixed-payoff framing changes bids enough to affect the comparison.

Baseline limits

  • Synthetic private values and public fixed risk scores
  • No common financial values or future rounds
  • No strategic score manipulation or endogenous arrival
  • The multi-slot 0.8v bid rule is assumed, not solved as equilibrium
  • The exact 0.75v BNE belongs only to the separate four-bidder single-slot benchmark
  • Lower fixed PM ID breaks exact ties in arrivals, risk scores, and bids
